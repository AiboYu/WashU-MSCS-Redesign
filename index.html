01 Overview

A graduate degree built for software-focused careers

The Master of Science in Computer Science is a 30-unit program for students who already have a computer science background and want to deepen their technical skills through advanced coursework. You can complete the degree through coursework alone, or extend it with a supervised project or research thesis.

The program is intentionally flexible: the curriculum is organized around a small set of breadth requirements that ensure depth in theory, software systems, and machines or applications, while leaving most of your degree open to electives you choose.

Prerequisites

CSE 1301 and CSE 2407 (or equivalents) must be completed, tested out of, or finished at the start of the program.

Thesis

A two-semester research thesis (CSE 7998, 6 units) with 24 units of coursework.

12 6 12
  • CSE 5000-level coursework · 12 units
  • Thesis · 6 units
  • Electives · 12 units

Grading: 24 units graded, C− or better; thesis units follow the capstone grading policy.

After a successful defense, breadth is waived — all 18 CSE units may be any 5000-level type. A transcript notation records the thesis.

Shared requirements

  • 18 CSE units at the 5000 level or higher.
  • Project and thesis units (CSE 7998) count toward those 18 CSE units only after a successful defense.
  • 12 elective units in CSE at the 4000 or 5000 level.
  • Out-of-department work: up to 6 units may count toward electives with Graduate Coordinator approval.
  • Excluded: independent study (CSE 400/4000, CSE 500/5999) and courses marked “N” do not count.
  • Minimum GPA: 2.70 cumulative, per School of Engineering policy.

Breadth requirement

Of the 18 CSE units, 9 units must satisfy breadth: one Theory (T) course, one Software Systems (S) course, and one Machines (M) or Applications (A) course. See the official course list below.

A note on capstone grading

The program page describes capstone grading in two ways: the general requirements list project and thesis units (CSE 7998) as pass/no-pass, while the capstone section says CSE 7998 is letter-graded each semester, with C− or higher required to continue a 6-unit sequence. We have not resolved this discrepancy. Consult the Master's Program Handbook or the Graduate Program Coordinator for the definitive policy.

03 Curriculum & breadth

Depth across theory, systems, and machines

The breadth requirement is designed to keep every graduate broad enough to be dangerous. Each category maps to a defined set of 5000-level courses:

  • T

    Theory

    Algorithms, complexity, and formal foundations of computing.

  • S

    Software Systems

    Operating systems, compilers, networks, and large-scale software.

  • M

    Machines

    Hardware, architecture, and systems that touch the physical world.

  • A

    Applications

    Machine learning, AI, graphics, and other applied areas.

The capstone process

Since Fall 2025, project and thesis students register for CSE 7998. The path runs: proposal → committee approval → mid-point progress report → defense → final report. A two-semester project or thesis requires a progress report at the end of semester one.

04 Research opportunities

Turn coursework into research

The thesis path is the structured route into research, but a project can also connect you to a faculty member's lab. The department spans eleven research areas — from machine learning and artificial intelligence to cybersecurity, networked systems, parallel computing, and theory.

05 Admissions

What you need to apply

Applications open through McKelvey Engineering. A complete application includes transcripts from all schools attended, three letters of recommendation (full-time), a statement of purpose (1–2 pages), and a résumé or CV.

  • Application fee — $75, non-refundable (waived for current WashU students/employees and info-session attendees).
  • Transcripts — all undergraduate and graduate institutions.
  • Recommendations — 3 letters (full-time) or 2 letters (part-time).
  • Statement of purpose — 1–2 pages.
  • Résumé / CV.
  • GRE — optional for full-time; not required for part-time. Submitting scores “will neither help nor hurt.”

English proficiency (international applicants)

  • TOEFL iBT — 90 overall (prior scale) / 5.0 overall (current scale), including Home Edition.
  • IELTS Academic — 6.5.
  • Duolingo — 125.

Official scores must arrive by the application deadline. There is no stated minimum GPA.

Funding — competitive, not guaranteed

Several sources of support exist, but none is guaranteed; most depend on merit or faculty research funding and do not require a separate application.

  • Master's Excellence Scholarship — merit-based, up to $16,000.
  • Bachelor's/Master's Scholarship — 50% tuition for eligible WashU undergraduates.
  • Assistantships — Chair's Fellowship, MS Research Assistantship, and Master's Fellowship (faculty-funded).
  • Federal aid — Direct Unsubsidized Loan (up to $20,500/year) and Work-Study via the FAFSA (school code 002520), for eligible domestic students.

International students should contact the financial aid office directly about options. Email mckelveygradfinancialaid@wustl.edu or call 314-935-1155.

07 Frequently asked questions

Common questions

What are the application deadlines?

Full-time: Fall deadline March 1 (early decision Dec 31); Spring deadline October 1. Part-time: Fall July 15, Spring December 1.

Is the GRE required?

No. The GRE is optional for full-time applicants and not required for part-time applicants.

What English scores do I need?

TOEFL iBT 90 (prior scale) or 5.0 (current scale), IELTS 6.5, or Duolingo 125.

Can I do research as a master's student?

Yes — the thesis path is the structured research route, and a project can connect you with a faculty lab.

What's the difference between a project and a thesis?

A thesis is research-oriented and, after a successful defense, waives the breadth requirement. A project is more flexible in scope and counts as 5000-level coursework (3 or 6 units).

Is funding guaranteed?

No. Scholarships and assistantships are competitive and depend on merit or faculty funding. Contact the financial aid office for specifics.

Do I need a computer science background?

The program expects a computing background. Prerequisites CSE 1301 and CSE 2407 (or equivalents) must be completed, tested out of, or finished at the start of the program.
